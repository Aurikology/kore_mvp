The problem

Strain is the thing students are actually managing.

Not motivation, not time — strain. It accumulates quietly across a term, and the two things most students reach for to push through it, caffeine and nicotine, are chosen because they are fast and available, not because they work well.

  • You can't see it coming

    By the time a study block has gone bad, it has usually been going bad for a while. There is no dashboard for the thing that is degrading.

  • The alternatives ask too much

    Meditation apps demand effort and attention from someone who has run out of both. That is the wrong moment to ask for twenty minutes of practice.

  • Nothing proves it worked

    A timer that plays audio at you cannot tell you whether anything changed. Without a measurement, every reset is a guess you have to take on faith.

The loop

Detect. Reset. Confirm. Reinforce.

The product is the loop, not the sensor. Every step below is running in the desktop prototype today — against a simulated signal source, which is the part that is not real yet.

  1. 01 / DETECT

    A personal baseline, then a number

    The first 15 seconds capture your own resting ratio. After that the Cognitive Load Index updates four times a second, scored against that baseline rather than against a population average.

    Latched at 70 held five seconds, released at 60 — so the state doesn't flicker at the boundary.

  2. 02 / RESET

    Sixty seconds, guided

    One protocol, box breathing, one minute long. Short enough that a person mid-collapse will actually start it, and the meter keeps running behind it the whole time.

    No content library, no streaming audio, nothing to choose from.

  3. 03 / CONFIRM

    One question, paired with the data

    Afterwards KORE asks how clear you feel, one to five. That answer is stored next to the measured change in the index, so what you felt and what was measured stay attached to each other.

    Both halves are kept, even when they disagree.

  4. 04 / REINFORCE

    Trends you can argue with

    Day streak, mean index drop, mean clarity. Enough to see whether the habit is holding without turning your attention into another thing to optimise.

    History is written to a file on your machine. There is no server to send it to.

The measurement

The signal processing is real. The electrode isn't — yet.

This is the part worth being specific about, because it is the difference between a focus product and a meditation timer with a gauge drawn on it. Here is the whole chain, from sample to number.

  1. Samples 256 Hz
    microvolts
  2. DC blocker one-pole
    0.5 Hz
  3. Mains notch RBJ biquad
    60 Hz, Q = 20
  4. Goertzel bank 512-sample Hann
    hop 64 → 4 frames/s
  5. Band power theta 4.0–7.5 Hz
    alpha 8.0–12.0 Hz
  6. Load index logistic
    0–100
Cognitive Load Index
r = ln( Pθ / Pα )
CLI = 100 / (1 + exp( −((r − μ) − C) / K ))
K = 1.6  C = 1.6  μ = your baseline

Why a ratio

Impedance, amplifier gain and windowing constants all cancel in a theta-to-alpha ratio. That means the number tracks you rather than the state of the contact — the single most fragile thing about any wearable sensor.

Why Goertzel, not FFT

Only 17 of 256 bins matter here. A Goertzel bin is exactly the corresponding DFT bin, so we compute the seventeen we need and skip the rest. The 512-sample periodic Hann window at 256 Hz gives exactly 0.5 Hz bin spacing.

Calibrated, not eyeballed

Power is normalised one-sided and window-corrected. A 50 µV 10 Hz tone reads alpha = 1250 µV², and a test in the repository asserts that figure. Two engines compute it — a Dart reference and a native C++ implementation over FFI — held to 1e-9 relative agreement, with automatic fallback to Dart if the native library fails to load.

What this does not show

The electrode is simulated. These numbers describe the pipeline's behaviour, not a person's. They are evidence that the filters, the transform and the index are correct and monotonic — not evidence about anyone's attention.

For the same reason, the drop you see during a reset in the prototype is arithmetic over a simulation. Real recovery physiology only becomes measurable when a real sensor is on the other end, and that is honest work still ahead of us.

Honesty rules

Three rules that make the number harder to fake.

It would be trivial to make KORE's statistics look good. These three constraints are written into the prototype specifically to stop that, and they cost us numbers on purpose.

  1. A reset only counts if you finished it

    The check-in is offered only when the protocol ran to the end. Abandon it halfway and nothing is recorded — a skipped reset is not a data point, and it will not pad a streak.

  2. No baseline, no claim

    Resets that start before the 15-second calibration completes are not logged at all. Without your baseline the index has nothing to be relative to, so we would rather record nothing than record something meaningless.

  3. The summary reports the number that happened

    The post-reset sheet states the measured change plainly, including the sessions where the index went up. A system that only ever shows you improvement is not measuring anything.

Where the project actually is

Built and not built, in plain language.

KORE is an early prototype and we would rather you know exactly which half you are looking at.

Working today

  • A desktop app that runs. Flutter on Windows, zero plugins, bundled fonts.
  • The full signal chain. Filters, Goertzel bank, band power and the index, in a Dart reference implementation and a native C++ one.
  • 52 passing tests, including the µV² calibration assertion and 1e-9 parity between the two engines.
  • The whole loop end to end. Baseline, index, reset protocol, check-in, streaks and recovery trends.
  • A hardware seam. The sensor sits behind one interface, so a real source can drop in without touching the engine, the index or the interface.

Not built yet

  • Hardware. There is no patch, no headband and no Bluetooth link. Nothing speaks to a device, and nothing is for sale.
  • Real physiological recovery data. The uplift the prototype shows is computed over a simulated signal.
  • A mobile app. The Android target is a scaffold. The phone is the plan, not the present.
  • Accounts, sync, cloud anything. No server exists. Today that is a limitation; it is also why your data has nowhere to leak to.
  • Prediction. KORE measures the load you are under now. Forecasting a crash before it lands is the next problem, not a feature.

Direction · designed, not shipped

Where this is going.

Stated as intent, because none of it exists. Read it as the plan we are working against, not as a product description.

  1. A wearable EEG patch

    Edge sensing, streaming over Bluetooth into the sensor interface the prototype already defines. This is the piece that turns every number on this page from a pipeline property into a personal one.

  2. The phone as the main surface

    Desktop stays as the development and demo surface. The point of a screenless ritual is that you are not sitting in front of a dashboard for it.

  3. Forecasting instead of only measuring

    Moving from "your load is high right now" to a useful warning earlier, with thresholds personalised across sessions rather than re-learned every time you open the app.

  4. Longitudinal history, if you want it

    Cross-device history and longer-run trends. Local-only remains the default; anything that leaves your machine should be a decision you made.

Pilot

We're looking for a first cohort, not customers.

The goal for the first round is a small group of students using the prototype daily for two to three weeks, so we can find out whether the loop is worth returning to before we build anything you would wear. If that sounds like something you would take part in — or you work with students and want to help run it — we would like to hear from you.

To say the obvious: there is nothing to buy, no waiting list for a device, and no device.
